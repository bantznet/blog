I recently had to replace my long-lived LG TV, which had died after something like 10 years - not a bad run these days. Off the Costco I went, and what I wasn't expecting was _just how inexpensive new TVs are_ for the quality you get. Surely there's a reason why, right? Right: marketing. 

Being a nerd, I was dead-set on a Google TV device, sadly acknowledging that I am ultimately the product for said corporation. 

And then I found [CleanYourTV](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv).

In what I can only think is a wonderful anathema to enshittification, _released not even a month ago as of the writing of this post_. From the repo:

> On 17 October 2026 I posted [a before and after](https://x.com/gathint/status/2111379569030939102) of my parents' TV, cleaned up by Claude Code over Wi-Fi. It reached 8.6M views, people started doing the same to their own TVs, and their replies turned into the lists here: 170+ TV models from 200+ contributors in the first four days. The repo was called tv-vibehack until the 19th; old links work.
>
> Two weeks on, it was the #1 trending repository on GitHub for the week of 19–25 October, with over 46K stars and 5.7K forks, and 1.4M views and 260K clones in 14 days. By 14 November it had 57K stars and 7.4K forks. The lists now cover 481 TV models from 131 brands. The coverage: 546 articles from 57 countries and 674 videos on YouTube and Bilibili, all in [docs/PRESS.md](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv/blob/main/docs/PRESS.md) (numbers as of 14 November 2026).

I've since found another project, [Couch TV](https://github.com/Seagey/couch-tv), that I feel tries to take owning what your TV runs and does for you a little further - I tried both, and have settled on what I see as the more simple, CleanYourTV.

What a time to be ~~so incredibly reliant on AI~~ alive :-)
